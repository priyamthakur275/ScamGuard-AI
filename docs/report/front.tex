\chapter*{\centering Abstract}
SMS remains a primary vector for financial fraud in India. Fraudulent messages imitate bank KYC reminders, electricity disconnection notices, parcel updates and job offers. Public smishing collections consist only of user-reported messages and lack a verified genuine class, so models trained on them learn to treat genuine bank messages as scams.

This report presents ScamGuard, an AI-based system that classifies a message as scam or genuine, identifies the phrases that influenced the decision and recommends a safe course of action. The detector combines logistic regression over word and character TF-IDF features with 31 interpretable intent patterns.

We construct ScamGuard-IN-2026, a corpus of 11,399 Indian messages drawn from public smishing reports, real Indian SMS, 2025--26 advisory texts and synthetic messages restricted to training. An audit shows that 19.5\% of the retained reports are in fact genuine bank alerts, OTP notices or advertisements. Near-duplicate messages are clustered into campaigns using MinHash, and each campaign is assigned entirely to either the training or the test partition to prevent leakage.

On 1,874 real messages from unseen campaigns, the system achieves 97.1\% accuracy with a 3.2\% false-alarm rate. Trained on the unaudited report labels instead, the classifier's false-alarm rate rises from 2.6\% to 34.0\%.

A cross-domain study shows that a filter trained on the UK-based UCI SMS Spam Collection drops from a spam F1 of 0.911 to 0.678 on Indian SMS. Adding only 10\% of a labelled Indian pool (143 messages) restores the F1 to 0.964. ScamGuard is deployed as a web application with a Streamlit demonstration.
